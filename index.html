Service

Guest Service Guidelines

Step 1 — Guest Welcome

Responsible: Hostess / Supervisor

  • Escort the guest to the table.
  • Greet them with a welcome: “Welcome to City Milano.”
  • Ask whether they would like still or sparkling water.
  • Present the menu.
  • Place a handbag holder beside female guests.
  • If children are present, bring a high chair, if available.
  • If the booking includes a birthday or anniversary, notify the responsible waiter so they can arrange a complimentary dessert with a candle or a complimentary welcome glass.

Step 2 — Taking the Order

Responsible: Chef de Rang

  • Introduce the menu.
  • Suggest a cocktail or a glass of wine, such as a glass of sparkling wine to start.
  • Present the meat selection and the fresh fish of the day.
  • Offer to accompany the guest to the fish and meat display, taking care not to cause congestion in the dining room.
  • Advise guests of the longer waiting times for whole-cooked fish of the day and meat dishes.
  • Check whether guests have any allergies or intolerances.
  • Take the order at the table.

To be defined: How long to wait before taking the order.

Step 3 — Wine Service

  • Bring the bottle to the guest’s left and show the label to the person who ordered it.
  • Use the panadora or tray as a base when opening the bottle.
  • Pour a tasting for the person who ordered the wine and wait for approval before serving the other guests.
  • Pour from the guest’s right, starting with the women, then the men, and proceeding clockwise.
  • When the bottle is empty, ask whether the guests would like another.
  • If it is the last bottle available, inform the guest before opening it.
  • Waiters are responsible for pouring water and wine throughout the service.

Step 4 — Bread and Olive Oil

Responsible: Commis

  • Serve bread and olive oil before the amuse-bouche.
  • Explain the selection; for details about the types of bread and olive oil, see the Food Bible.

Step 5 — Amuse-Bouche

  • Serve after the bread and olive oil.
  • Scrambled egg served in its shell, with Parmesan fondue and shavings of black truffle.

Step 6 — Place Setting

Reference: See the technical sheet.

Raw dishes: Provide a show plate and napkin for all sizes. Remove the pine cone, if necessary. For sharing, bring sharing plates and prepare the clips.

Seafood platter: Bring hand towels for guests to use at the end of the meal.

Caviar: Provide a butter knife for the whole table. Always serve with an ivory spoon.

Fish sold by weight: Two staff members serve the fish: one portions it and the other serves it.

Meat: See the specifications below.

Note — Meat cut specifications

  • Manzetta Prussiana: portions under 500 g are not sold.
  • Fiorentina: minimum 1,000 g.
  • Argentinian fillet: from 200–250 g upwards.
  • Tomahawk: approximately 1.5 kg.
  • Other cuts of meat: in 200 g increments.
  • Meat sold by weight is always served with grills so guests can finish cooking it at the table.

Step 7 — Clearing and Changing Cutlery Between Courses

  • Clear the plates as soon as each course is finished.
  • Change the cutlery according to the next course, referring to the Food Bible for appropriate pairings.
  • Keep clips and tongs ready for handling sharing plates.

Step 8 — Pre-Dessert Table Clearing

  • Clear the table completely before serving dessert.
  • Prepare the table with a stand for the petits fours (pre-dessert), to be served before the dessert ordered by the guests.

Step 9 — Petits Fours

  • Always serve the petits fours, whether or not the table orders dessert.
  • Serve them before the ordered dessert, or instead of it if the guests do not order dessert.

Step 10 — The Bill

  • Present the review card with the bill.

Step 11 — Farewell

  • Escort the guest to the exit.
  • Say goodbye: “Thank you for dining with us / for visiting us. We wish you a lovely evening / the rest of your evening.”
  • Clear the table within five minutes of the guests’ departure and reset it for the next service.

End of Evening — Closing the Shift
